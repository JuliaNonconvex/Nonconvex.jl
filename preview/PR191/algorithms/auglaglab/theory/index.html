Theory: augmented Lagrangians, PBM and quasi-Newton sub-solvers

The augmented Lagrangian method

For the equality-constrained problem $\min f(x)$ s.t. $c(x) = 0$, the augmented Lagrangian (Hestenes; Powell; Rockafellar) is

\[L_\rho(x, y) = f(x) + y^\top c(x) + \tfrac{\rho}{2}\lVert c(x)\rVert^2.\]

The outer loop alternates:

  1. Primal step: $x^{k+1} \approx \arg\min_x L_{\rho_k}(x, y^k)$.
  2. Multiplier update: $y^{k+1} = y^k + \rho_k\, c(x^{k+1})$.

The update rule is forced by stationarity: the KKT system of the original problem pairs $\nabla f + J^\top y = 0$ with $c = 0$, and the subproblem stationarity $\nabla f(x^{k+1}) + J^\top (y^k + \rho_k c(x^{k+1})) = 0$ shows the parenthesized quantity is the next multiplier estimate. Rockafellar's interpretation: the ALM is the proximal point method on the dual function, which is why it converges under far weaker assumptions than the pure penalty method — the penalty $\rho$ no longer needs to tend to infinity, and a constant, large-enough $\rho$ suffices for local convergence. Inequality constraints enter through the max-form $g(x) \le 0 \leadsto \lambda^\top \max(g,0) + \frac{\rho}{2}\lVert\max(g,0)\rVert^2$ with the update $\lambda^{k+1} = \max(0, \lambda^k + \rho_k g(x^{k+1}))$.

The practical ALM of Birgin–Martínez

AugLagAlg follows the practical scheme of Birgin & Martínez (ALGENCAN):

  • Feasibility-driven penalty. The penalty is increased only when the feasibility did not improve enough. Two tolerance sequences drive the loop: $\eta_k$ (feasibility target — a step is "good" when $\lVert v(x)\rVert_\infty \le \eta_k$) and $\omega_k$ (how accurately the subproblem must be solved). After a good step both tighten with $1/\rho$; after a bad step the penalty grows geometrically and the tolerances reset.
  • Multiplier safeguards. The multipliers are updated only on good steps, avoiding the classical failure where noisy multipliers from poorly solved subproblems destabilize the next one.
  • Two-way adaptation (Curtis–Gould–Jiang–Robinson). A monotonically increasing penalty makes the subproblems ill-conditioned. When the multiplier update is small and feasibility improved, the penalty is allowed to decrease, keeping the subproblems tractable.
  • Infeasibility detection. If the penalty reaches its cap while the violation stagnates, the algorithm stops rather than looping forever.
  • Least-squares warm start (lsq_multipliers): the first multipliers solve $\min_\lambda \lVert \nabla f(x^0) + J(x^0)^\top \lambda \rVert^2$, a far better estimate than zero for equality-constrained problems.

The penalty-barrier-multiplier method (PBM)

The classical ALM treats violations quadratically. The PBM of Ben-Tal & Zibulevsky (used by PENNON) replaces the inequality term with a shifted logarithmic barrier:

\[F(x, u, c) = f(x) + \sum_i c_i \log\!\left(1 + \frac{u_i g_i(x)}{c_i}\right), \qquad \frac{u_i g_i(x)}{c_i} > -1,\]

with the multiplier update $u_i^{k+1} = u_i^k \,/\, (1 + u_i^k g_i(x^k)/c_i)$ and a penalty increase when progress stalls. Stationarity of the subproblem gives $\nabla f + \sum_i \frac{u_i}{1 + u_i g_i / c_i} \nabla g_i = 0$, so the effective multiplier is $\lambda_i = u_i/(1 + u_i g_i/c_i)$ — the fixed point of the update is exactly the KKT complementarity, and on the feasible side ($g_i < 0$) the multipliers grow, sharpening the barrier near the boundary. In AugLagAlg the PBM term applies on the feasible side while the infeasible side falls back to the classical quadratic term — the standard globalization of the barrier domain (penalty = :pbm, or :mixed for quadratic semidefinite terms).

Projected ALM for semidefinite constraints

With a cone constraint $P(x) \succeq 0$ and a matrix multiplier $Y \succeq 0$, the projected augmented Lagrangian (Sun–Sun–Zhang) is

\[\varphi(x; Y, \rho) = \frac{1}{2\rho}\Big(\lVert \Pi_{\mathbb{S}_+^n}(Y - \rho P(x))\rVert_F^2 - \lVert Y\rVert_F^2\Big),\]

with the multiplier update $Y^{k+1} = \Pi_{\mathbb{S}_+^n}(Y^k - \rho_k P(x^{k+1}))$. The squared-distance-to-cone penalty is C¹ with gradient $-\langle \Pi_{\mathbb{S}_+^n}(Y - \rho P(x)), \nabla P(x)\rangle$, which NonconvexSemidefinite's project_psd rrule differentiates exactly. The scheme inherits the local (super)linear convergence rates of the classical ALM, and the subproblems are smooth NLPs solvable by any of the sub-solvers — including the built-in quasi-Newton driver.

Quasi-Newton sub-solvers

When every constraint is relaxed, each ALM subproblem is a smooth box-constrained NLP, solved by the built-in QuasiNewtonAlg. The theory:

  • Secant equation. A quasi-Newton model $B$ of the Hessian must satisfy $B_{k+1} s_k = y_k$ with $s_k = x^{k+1} - x^k$ and $y_k = \nabla f^{k+1} - \nabla f^k$.
  • BFGS. The minimal-norm update preserving symmetry and the secant equation is $B_{k+1} = B_k + \frac{y y^\top}{y^\top s} - \frac{B_k s s^\top B_k}{s^\top B_k s}$, with the Sherman–Morrison–Woodbury inverse update $H_{k+1} = (I - \rho s y^\top) H_k (I - \rho y s^\top) + \rho s s^\top$, $\rho = 1/(y^\top s)$ — the default :inverse form stores $H_k$, making the Newton step a single matrix–vector product; form = :hessian stores $B_k$ and factorizes.
  • Powell damping. If $y^\top s \le 0$ (nonconvex curvature), $y$ is blended toward $B_k s$ so the updated model stays positive definite.
  • L-BFGS. The two-loop recursion computes $B_k^{-1} g$ (and $B_k v$) from the last $m$ pairs in $O(mn)$ without storing a matrix; the base operator $H_0$ is the preconditioner (IdentityPreconditioner, DiagonalPreconditioner, or a user-defined operator).
  • Structured/sparse BFGS. The dense BFGS update destroys sparsity. The structure-preserving update of Fletcher restricts the dense update to the structural nonzeros of the container, so a sparse (or user-defined) Hessian keeps its pattern and its factorization works unchanged.
  • Trust region. The subproblem $\min_{\lVert d\rVert \le \Delta} g^\top d + \frac12 d^\top B d$ is solved with the Steihaug–Toint conjugate-gradient method, which terminates on the boundary or on negative curvature — robust for indefinite models. Paired with NewtonTRApprox (the exact Hessian through NonconvexCore's AD backends) this is the Newton trust-region method; the step is projected onto the box and accepted by the ratio of actual to predicted reduction.
  • Globalization. Line searches use Armijo backtracking along the projected direction; bounds are respected by projection, with the standard caveat that a projected step can stall on the boundary — the driver then shrinks the step or the radius.

The whole machinery is user-extensible through the AbstractHessianApprox interface: init!, apply!, update!, solve! and reset! — a custom matrix type with custom secant accumulation and a custom factorization plugs in directly.
