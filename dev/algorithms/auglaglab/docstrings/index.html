NonconvexAugLagLab.AbstractCurvatureTerm — Type
AbstractCurvatureTerm

An exact Hessian term of the (augmented Lagrangian) objective with a matrix-vector product apply_term!(Hv, term, v). Terms are position dependent and are rebuilt at each subproblem iterate through the composite's build_terms hook.

NonconvexAugLagLab.AbstractHessianApprox — Type
AbstractHessianApprox

A quasi-Newton Hessian approximation with a possibly custom structure and custom linear solves. See the module documentation for the extension API.

NonconvexAugLagLab.AugLagAlg — Type
AugLagAlg(sub_alg = nothing)

Augmented Lagrangian meta-algorithm. sub_alg solves the subproblems after the relaxed constraints are folded into the objective; nothing selects the built-in QuasiNewtonAlg, which requires every constraint to be relaxed. Relaxing semidefinite constraints additionally requires NonconvexSemidefinite to be loaded (its extension provides the cone projections).

NonconvexAugLagLab.AugLagOptions — Type
AugLagOptions(; kwargs...)
  • sub_options: options for the sub-solver. For the built-in QuasiNewtonAlg this is a named tuple of its keyword arguments (default: (; max_iter = 800, tol = 1e-8, globalization = :trustregion) — trust-region Steihaug–Toint steps are substantially more robust than backtracking on the nonconvex augmented Lagrangian subproblems); for a custom sub_alg it is forwarded as its options.
  • relaxed: a RelaxConstraintSelection (default: relax everything).
  • penalty: :quadratic (classical ALM), :pbm (penalty-barrier-multiplier for inequalities, quadratic for equalities), :mixed (PBM for inequalities, projected quadratic for semidefinite), :logdet (logdet nonlinear rescaling for semidefinite, quadratic elsewhere) or :mixed_logdet (PBM for inequalities, logdet for semidefinite). The logdet kernels are experimental: measured on the covariance MLE they converge but land short of the projected quadratic.
  • auto_rho_init: (opt-in) calibrate the initial penalty from the problem at x0: ρ = clamp(‖∇f(x0)‖ / max(‖c̃(x0)‖, ε), 1, 1e6) on the scaled constraints — the penalty gradient then balances the objective gradient, avoiding the measured escalation waste (the default rho_init = 10 needs ~130 outer iterations of escalation on AC-OPF). Measured: wins Rosetta AC-OPF case5 (viol 0.34 vs 0.60 at 200 outer iterations) but loses the TopOpt local-stress problem badly (near-feasible starts give a tiny scaled violation and an over-large penalty: viol 0.009 vs 5.7e-6, 7× slower) — opt-in for infeasible-start problems, off by default.
  • rho_init, rho_max, rho_incr, rho_decr: initial/maximum penalty and the increase/decrease factors (two-way adaptation).
  • max_iter: maximum outer iterations.
  • ctol: feasibility tolerance.
  • eta0, omega0: initial feasibility/optimality targets of the subproblems.
  • weights: per-constraint-group penalty weights (default: all ones).
  • lsq_multipliers: initialize equality multipliers from the least-squares stationarity residual (default true).
  • prox: (opt-in) proximal regularization of the AL subproblems (Rockafellar): adds τ/2‖x − x̄‖² to the subproblem objective, anchored at the outer iterate — conditions the ill-conditioned subproblems of the escalated penalty. 0 (default) disables it. Composes with every penalty mode (:bm, :pbm, :mixed, :logdet, :mixed_logdet) and with curvature = :sr1; with curvature = :newton, the exact-Hessian subproblems add the τI term (the prox term's true curvature). Only the two_level inner loop rebuilds from the un-proxed objective, so its inner anchor replaces the outer prox. Measured: 10% faster with slightly better feasibility on Rosetta AC-OPF case5 (151.7 vs 169.2 s, viol 0.584 vs 0.599), and on TopOpt local-stress 32% faster and more feasible on the single-level solve but converging to a heavier design (obj 0.181 vs 0.094 — the anchor bias), while the 3-stage SIMP continuation flips that trade: 2.7× faster (204 vs 560 s) and a lighter final design (obj 0.186 vs 0.187) at viol 5.3e-9 — opt-in. Combinations measured on Rosetta case5: with penalty = :pbm, prox is 13% faster and lands on a lower f but less feasible (159.5 s, viol 0.524 vs 183.4 s, 0.399 — no dominant win); with curvature = :sr1, prox is 27% faster but far from the optimum (57.6 s, f = 14187, viol 0.158 vs 78.9 s, f = 17576, viol 0.016) — the anchor counteracts SR1's curvature exploration, so the combination is not recommended.
  • memory: L-BFGS memory of the built-in subproblem solver (default 50; the curvature history is reused across outer iterations).
  • curvature: the curvature approximation of the built-in subproblem solver, :lbfgs (default), :sr1 (limited-memory symmetric rank-one — captures indefiniteness, intended for the trust-region subproblems), :newton (experimental, opt-in: exact-Hessian Newton trust-region solves — MathOptInterface evaluator Hessians for JuMP-converted models, AD otherwise, with the stale-Cholesky preconditioned Steihaug CG; the box-barrier mode auto-pairs with it) or :hybrid (experimental, opt-in: the trust-region Hessian model sums the exact Gauss-Newton penalty terms ρw·JᵀJ of the relaxed constraint groups — matrix-free through one forward finite difference and one reverse pass each, with the inequality rows restricted to the active set frozen at the subproblem iterate — plus an L-BFGS model of the remainder (λ-weighted terms; the composite's quasi-Newton part covers the remainder with the structured-secant update (Dennis–Gay–Welsch: the exact terms' curvature is subtracted from the secant pairs, so the QN models only the remainder), and the identity base operator is used since the penalty scale is already exact in the terms). Experimental; measured on the TopOpt local-stress single-level solve: the matvec cost dominates (a forward finite difference + a reverse pass per CG iteration against full-FEA stress constraints) — no completion within 20 min vs the 4.5 min L-BFGS baseline; on Rosetta case5 the JuMP-converted blocks use the exact sparse evaluator Jacobian (two sparse matvecs per CG iteration) and, with the second_order labels, converge to machine-precision feasibility in 29 outer iterations / 34.3 s (see second_order below) — faster than SR1 (80 s) and Newton (43.8 s), at a 1.4% higher objective (a different feasible local solution). The remainder-only L-BFGS inverse was also measured as a Steihaug CG preconditioner and is deliberately not wired (it stalled the solve on case5). Opt-in.
  • curvature = :split (experimental, opt-in): the split-secant model for problems without exact Hessians — the penalty part φ = ρ/2‖c‖² + ρ/2‖g⁺‖² and the remainder ψ = f + yᵀc + λᵀg⁺ get separate L-BFGS histories: the penalty pair (s, ∇φ(x⁺) − ∇φ(x)) is the exact directional second derivative of the dominant penalty curvature from gradient differences alone (two reverse passes of the scalar penalty function per accepted step, no Hessian-vector products), the remainder receives the structured-secant corrected pair (s, y − Δ∇φ) (Dennis–Gay–Welsch in gradient-difference form), the CG model is the sum of the two two-loop recursions and the penalty model's inverse is the Steihaug preconditioner. Quadratic penalty mode, no elastic mode, any model (no JuMP requirement). Measured on the TopOpt local-stress single-level solve: no completion within 40 min vs the 4.5 min L-BFGS baseline — the two penalty-part reverse passes per accepted step (full FEA adjoints) dominate; the design targets problems with cheap constraint evaluations.
  • curvature = :fdgrad (experimental, opt-in): inexact-Newton curvature from one-sided finite differences of the Zygote-computed AL gradient (Hv = (g(x+εv) − g(x))/ε, reusing the driver's current gradient so each Hessian-vector product costs one extra gradient evaluation; Zygote cannot be nested and ForwardDiff cannot cross its tape); pair with cg_maxiter (via sub_options) for a small product budget. Measured on the TopOpt local-stress single-level solve: no completion within 15 min at cg_maxiter = 5 vs the 4.5 min L-BFGS baseline — the per-CG-iteration gradient evaluations dominate on FEA-heavy constraints; a single CG iteration with the L-BFGS preconditioner (cg_maxiter = 1, precond = true, plain :lbfgs) also loses (no completion within 28 min: steepest-descent-quality steps). Measured: :sr1 is a massive- second_order: (opt-in, with curvature = :hybrid) a SecondOrderSelection selecting which relaxed constraint groups contribute their exact multiplier-weighted curvature μᵀ∇²c (MathOptInterface Lagrangian-Hessian triplets, one sparse assembly per subproblem iterate) on top of the Gauss-Newton term; untagged groups keep the Gauss-Newton term only, with their multiplier curvature absorbed by the L-BFGS remainder. JuMP-converted blocks only. nothing (default) disables the exact terms. Measured on Rosetta case5: labeling all groups (with the structured-secant composite update) converges to machine-precision feasibility in 29 outer iterations / 34.3 s (viol 9.3e-14, f = 17808 — 1.4% above the 17553 optimum Newton finds, a different feasible local solution) vs 80 s for SR1 and 43.8 s for Newton.
  • inexact_cg: (opt-in) pass the subproblem tolerance to the built-in trust-region solver as a relative Steihaug CG residual tolerance (Dembo–Eisenstat–Steihaug inexact Newton: the CG stops once ‖r‖ ≤ sub_tol·‖r₀‖, so loose subproblems run few Hessian-vector products and the budget tightens to zero as the outer loop converges). false (default) keeps the exact CG. Measured on Rosetta case5 (hybrid + all labels): bit-for-bit the same trajectory at 3.6% less wall-clock (251.9 vs 261.4 s) — a small win, off by default pending the TopOpt characterization.
  • sub_options.cg_maxiter, sub_options.precond (for the built-in quasi-Newton solver): cap the Steihaug CG iterations and enable the L-BFGS two-loop as the CG preconditioner (off by default — the preconditioner changes the steps). Measured on the TopOpt stress solve: cg_maxiter = 1 + precond = true takes steepest-descent-quality steps (no completion within 28 min vs the 4.5 min baseline); the split model's penalty-inverse preconditioner also activates through precond = true. Rosetta AC-OPF case5 win (80 vs 219 s, viol 0.016 vs 0.60, f near the true optimum) but slower on the TopOpt local-stress problem (no completion within 31 min vs ~6 min for L-BFGS) — opt-in; :newton with box_barrier solves case5 to the true optimum (43.8 s, f = 17553, viol 3.5e-8) — second-order only, opt-in.
  • elastic: relax the equalities through slack variables, yᵀ(c−s) + ρ/2‖c−s‖² + μΣ√(sᵢ²+ε²), the elastic mode of the augmented Lagrangian (more robust to infeasible subproblems; default false).
  • elastic_mu: the slack penalty coefficient of the elastic mode.
  • warm_continue: reuse the previous run's final iterate, multipliers, per-group weights and quasi-Newton curvature when optimize! is called again on the same workspace (e.g. across the stages of a SIMP continuation, where the model or the hyper-parameters change between solves); the multipliers are rescaled to the new constraint scales, while ρ, η, ω, the filter and the Anderson history are re-initialized from the new options. Measured on the released 0.2.5 code (TopOpt local-stress SIMP continuation): warm continuation is consistently faster than cold restarts (26% on a coarse 3-step schedule, 10% on a fine 6-step one) and keeps the design at the stress threshold (violation 0.0 vs 0.005 on 3 steps, 0.003 vs 0.032 on 6 steps), while cold restarts end with a lighter objective but slightly violate the stress limit. Use it when feasibility or runtime dominate the continuation, not for a lower final objective.
  • h0: base operator of the built-in L-BFGS recursion, :penalty (the default diagonal 1/(1+ρ) scaling) or :identity. Re-measured on 0.2.5/0.2.6: :identity is 12% slower on single-shot TopOpt solves for a 1% better objective and better PGLib case5 feasibility (gap 0.10 vs 0.12, violation 1.02 vs 1.14), while on a warm 6-step SIMP continuation (warm_continue = true) it is 9% faster but loses 21% of the final objective (0.198 vs 0.163) with a 3× larger stress violation — the faster variant is not close enough in accuracy there, so :penalty stays the default.
  • hard_rho: the penalty weight (default 1e3, must be positive) for constraints kept hard with the built-in sub-solver (AugLagAlg() with a partially-relaxing RelaxConstraintSelection): the kept equality/inequality constraints are folded into the subproblem objective as the smooth quadratic penalty hard_rho/2·(‖c‖² + ‖g⁺‖²) (the ALM-for-relaxed / penalty-for-kept split) and removed from the submodel so the box-only quasi-Newton solver accepts it; the kept constraints' feasibility is traded against hard_rho (large values drive their violation to zero). External sub-solvers receive the kept constraints natively instead. Measured on the TopOpt local-stress single-level solve with the stress constraints kept hard: converges in one outer iteration (132.7 s) but leaves the expected hard_rho residual — max solid stress 5.7e-4 over the threshold at a 2.2× heavier design; the relaxed ALM is strictly better there, this path is for problems with a cheap-hard/expensive-soft split.
  • watchdog, watchdog_tau, watchdog_tol: Birgin–Martínez-style nonmonotone outer acceptance (default false). When enabled, a step is accepted only if its AL merit does not exceed the recursive-mean reference f̄ ← (1-τ)·f̄ + τ·merit (τ = watchdog_tau; the reference starts at the first accepted merit and is left unchanged on rejected steps, so repeated failures eventually escalate the penalty), with an optional relative slack merit ≤ (1 + watchdog_tol)·f̄ (default 0.0). Combined with filter, both acceptance tests must pass. Opt-in, measured mixed: on the TopOpt local-stress problem the trajectories are bit-for-bit identical to the default (the subsolver always returns merit-decreasing steps), on Rosetta AC-OPF case5 it is a wash (milestones within 1–3 outer iterations), but on cases 14/118 it hurts — with merit-increasing subproblem steps the watchdog rejects them outright and the iterate can freeze (case118 ends at the start point, f = 0, viol 23 vs 0.62 for the default). A positive watchdog_tol relaxes the rejection and is the intended setting for such problems. Kept as an opt-in safety net for subsolvers that reliably return bad steps.
  • anderson_ineq: (opt-in) extend the Anderson acceleration (currently equality-only) to the inequality multiplier map of the classical quadratic penalty: the unprojected update λ + ρw·g is mixed with the last two iterates (same depth-2 least-squares mixing as the equality block) and the mix is re-projected onto the nonnegative orthant (ADMM-style Anderson with re-projection). Inactive for the PBM penalty modes. Measured on the TopOpt local-stress single-level solve (current default config): a wall-clock wash (262.6 vs 264.6 s) with a 4.6% lighter feasible design (obj 0.0895 vs 0.0938, reproduced exactly across configurations) and a marginally larger final violation (6.2e-6 vs 5.7e-6) — no time win, so it stays off by default; the opt-in "light-design recipe". With anderson_depth = 5 the design is a further 0.2% lighter and the violation beats the default (obj 0.0893, viol 5.4e-6) at the same wall-clock.
  • anderson_depth: the history depth of the Anderson mixing (default 2, must be ≥ 1; 1 disables the mixing). Applies to both the equality map and, when anderson_ineq is enabled, the inequality map.
  • multiplier_relax: (opt-in) Eckstein-style adaptive relaxation of the multiplier step: when the subproblem stationarity residual at the accepted iterate (the projected gradient of the subproblem objective, the built-in solver's own stopping metric) exceeds the requested subproblem tolerance, the dual increment is scaled down by the achieved-accuracy ratio θ = clamp(sub_tol/‖res‖, 0.1, 1) — a loose inner solve cannot poison the multiplier estimates with a full step. Applies to the equality updates and the classical quadratic inequality updates (the Anderson residuals are scaled to match); the PBM/SD multiplier updates are left unscaled. One extra subproblem-gradient evaluation per outer iteration when enabled. Measured on the TopOpt local-stress single-level solve: 2.1% slower (309.5 vs 303.1 s) but a 2.1% lighter feasible design (obj 0.0918 vs 0.0938) with marginally better feasibility — a small quality-for-time trade, off by default.
  • spectral_rho: (opt-in) replace the two-way penalty decrease on good steps with a spectral (Barzilai–Borwein) update of the dual step: ρ ← ‖Δc‖²/⟨Δc, Δy⟩ from the last two iterates of the dual map (Xu–Figueiredo–Goldstein style), applied only on a credible positive correlation, limited to [ρ/rho_decr, ρ·rho_incr] and [rho_init/10, rho_max]. Equality and inequality multipliers participate (semidefinite matrices do not); the elastic mode is not supported (falls back to the two-way rule there). Escalation on bad steps is unchanged. Measured on the TopOpt local-stress single-level solve (current default config): 3.0% slower (272.6 vs 264.6 s) and a 1.3% heavier design (obj 0.0950 vs 0.0938) — a straight loss, off by default. (An earlier 6.2%-faster measurement was against a fixed-tolerance configuration and does not carry over.)
  • dual_secant: (opt-in) Broyden secant correction on the equality multiplier map (the Birgin–Martínez "improving ultimate convergence" device): the inverse secant model of the residual map of y += ρw·c is refined with the good-Broyden inverse update from the map's secant pairs, and the multiplier step becomes the quasi-Newton step y − C·(ρw·c) (the first step reproduces the plain ascent), safeguarded back to the plain ascent when the model step exceeds twice its norm. Replaces the plain update and the equality Anderson mixing when enabled; inactive for the elastic mode and without relaxed equalities. Awaiting the AC-OPF measurement (the TopOpt stress problem has no relaxed equalities, so the path is inert there).
  • filter, stall_retry, selective, safeguard, two_way, levenberg: toggles for the corresponding defaults (all true). Re-measured on 0.2.5: removing the selective weights costs ~79% TopOpt wall-clock, removing the multiplier safeguard degrades PGLib case14 (gap 0.19 → 0.28), removing the Levenberg damping trades ~30% TopOpt speed for a worse objective and feasibility; the filter, stall retry and the two-way ρ decrease are inert on the measured benchmarks (kept as safety nets).
  • sub_tol_schedule: :eta (the default), :eta2, :fixed (1e-8) or :relative. The default :eta schedule loosens the subproblem tolerance as the outer feasibility target tightens (clamped to [1e-6, 1e-2]): cheap sub-solves while the outer loop only needs rough steps, floored at 1e-6 for the final accuracy (measured 28% faster than :eta2 on TopOpt stress with 4.8x tighter final feasibility). :eta2 (clamped to [1e-10, 1e-8]) remains for maximal final accuracy. :relative sets the inner tolerance proportional to the current constraint violation, clamp(sub_tol_rel * v_prev, 1e-10, 1e-2) with sub_tol_rel (default 0.1, must be in (0, 1)): the Eckstein–Silva practical relative criterion — the subproblem is solved only to an accuracy comparable to the current infeasibility, so effort concentrates where it pays. Measured on the TopOpt local-stress single-level solve: bit-for-bit the same trajectory as the :eta schedule at 3% more wall-clock (290.9 vs 282.2 s) — no TopOpt win, so :eta stays the default. The first subproblem is tightened separately by first_sub_tol (see below). The schedule governs every sub-solver: external solvers (Ipopt, MMA, NLopt) receive the current tolerance through their options via the package extensions (_set_sub_tol).
  • first_sub_tol: the first subproblem is solved to min(sub_tol, first_sub_tol) (default 1e-6); the first inner solve selects the local basin the whole solve follows (measured on the TopOpt local-stress problem: the schedule's loose first solve lands in a 4% heavier design's basin), so it is tightened while later subproblems keep the cheap schedule tolerance. Measured: the default :eta schedule plus first_sub_tol = 1e-6 reproduces the light-design trajectory of a fixed-1e-6 override bit-for-bit at 12.7% less wall-clock (264.6 vs 303.1 s). Set first_sub_tol = nothing for the pre-0.4.1 behavior.
  • first_sub_iters: how many of the leading subproblems get the first_sub_tol tightening (default 1, must be ≥ 1). Measured on the TopOpt single-level solve: 2 is bit-for-bit inert (262.0 vs 264.6 s, identical trajectory) — the basin is selected by the first solve alone.
  • max_time: a wall-clock budget in seconds (default Inf): no new subproblem is started once the budget is exhausted, and the solve returns the last completed outer iterate.
  • on_iteration: (opt-in) a per-iteration callback on_iteration(iter, x, f(x), vnorm) called after every accepted outer iterate (with a copy of the iterate); returning true stops the loop and reports the just-recorded iterate.
  • analytical_gradient: (opt-in) assemble the built-in subproblem's gradient analytically as ∇f + Σ Jᵀv — one vector-Jacobian product per constraint group through the existing reverse-mode machinery, exposed via a ChainRules rrule (the SDP-extension pattern) — instead of differentiating the composed AL closure. Quadratic penalty mode only (no PBM, elastic or curvature = :newton). Measured on the TopOpt local-stress single-level solve: bit-for-bit the same trajectory as the composed-AD path at 17.8% more wall-clock (311.6 vs 264.6 s) — the per-group reverse passes cost more than the single composed pass on this one-group problem; off by default, an architecture/robustness option for multi-group problems.
  • stop_kkt, kkt_tol: opt-in KKT-complete stopping (default false, the historical feasibility-only test): with stop_kkt = true, once vnorm ≤ ctol the solve also requires the box-projected gradient of the scaled Lagrangian at the current multipliers to satisfy ≤ kkt_tol (default 1e-6) and the complementarity residual max |λⱼgⱼ| over the relaxed inequalities to satisfy ≤ kkt_tol. Costs one Lagrangian-gradient assembly per outer iteration once feasible. A feasibility-only solve would terminate at any feasible iterate — including the starting point of a feasible-start problem — so stationarity is available as an opt-in; the tolerance is set at the residual plateau measured on the TopOpt local-stress single-level solve (the multiplier-error scale is ~ρ·viol, so the Lagrangian residual stalls around 1e-4-1e-3 on that 1600-constraint problem, where the old 1e-6 default never fired within 100 outer iterations).
  • outer_schedule: :bm (the default) — the Birgin–Martínez complexity scaling where both the feasibility target η and the subproblem accuracy target ω tighten with the full 1/ρ factor on good steps (and reset with 1/ρ on escalation) — or :default (the gentler ρ^-0.9 / ρ^-0.1 exponents, the pre-0.2.10 behavior). Measured consistently faster: on the TopOpt local-stress single-level p = 3 solve it converges in 11 vs 13 outer iterations (312 vs 360 s) at a slightly worse final accuracy (viol 5.7e-6 vs 1.2e-6), and on Rosetta AC-OPF case5 it is 16% faster (217 vs 258 s) with marginally better feasibility (viol 0.366 vs 0.369).
  • scaling: normalize the relaxed constraints and the objective by their reference magnitudes, so the penalty applies to balanced, unit-scaled constraint violations (default true).
  • scale_mode: the constraint scale scheme — :gradient (default, the historical scheme) uses the Curtis–Jiang–Robinson-style finite-difference gradient-magnitude probe per group; :value scales each group/component by its reference magnitude max |c(x₀)| (unit-free residuals, penalty weights independent of the constraint's derivative scale, which can be unrelated to its values when the function involves a linear solve).
  • two_level, two_level_iters, two_level_tau: (experimental, opt-in) two-level semi-proximal inner loop for the semidefinite block (SDPNAL+-style sPADMM adapted to nonlinear P(x), plan S2 option B): after each subproblem solve, alternate the multiplier projection with two_level_iters prox-augmented subproblem solves (AL(x) + τ/2‖x − x̂‖²). Requires the built-in sub-solver and relaxed semidefinite constraints. Measured: 12× faster on the N=8 covariance MLE (1.9 vs 23.2 s, same 1.3e-8 accuracy) and 2.7× faster on the BMI (5.7 vs 15.6 s, with a more stable closed loop, λ = −1.07 vs −0.82).
  • box_barrier, box_barrier_scale: (experimental, opt-in) Ipopt-style barrierification of the subproblem's box bounds: the box enters the subproblem objective as a log barrier −μ Σ(log(x−l) + log(u−x)) with a C² extension outside, μ adapts by the implied-dual complementarity on the near-bound coordinates (Ipopt-style κ rule), the start is pushed off the bounds by the barrier thickness (bound-push), and the hard box stays projected in the subproblem (fraction-to-boundary at τ = 1) so the iterate never leaves the bounds. box_barrier = missing (the default) is automatic: on with curvature = :newton (measured on Rosetta case5: 43.8 s to f = 17553, viol 3.5e-8) and off with the first-order subproblems (measured harmful there: viol 0.66 vs 0.40).
  • pbm_adaptive: (opt-in) Ipopt-style barrier adaptation for the PBM penalty modes: each inequality group's barrier coefficient tracks the complementarity of its implied duals (grow the barrier when the complementarity exceeds the target, shrink it when well-complemented) instead of following the global penalty. Measured on Rosetta AC-OPF case5: ~10% faster (256 vs 284 s) at the same feasibility.
  • restore: (opt-in) Ipopt-style restoration phase: when the subproblem iterate blows up (non-finite or exploding violation) instead of just reverting to the previous point, solve a feasibility-only problem (min Σ‖c‖² + Σ‖g⁺‖² + Σ‖viol_sd‖² over the box, on the same scaled constraints as the main phase) with the built-in quasi-Newton driver to recover a usable iterate, then continue.
  • restore_stall: (opt-in, with restore) also trigger the restoration when feasibility stalls: no improvement of more than restore_tol over the last restore_stall outer iterations. The restored point replaces the iterate and the equality multipliers are re-estimated (least squares) there; at most 2 restorations per solve. 0 (default) keeps the blow-up-only trigger.
  • restore_tol: the feasibility-improvement threshold for the restore_stall trigger, in the units of restore_tol_mode: :relative (default 1e-2: the violation must have dropped by more than 1% over the window) or :absolute (a drop in the raw scaled violation). The main phase is not feasibility-preserving, so when a restoration point is strictly more feasible than the final main-phase iterate it is reported as the result. Measured on Rosetta AC-OPF case5: restore = true, restore_stall = 5 drives the violation from 0.60 to 1.6e-6 and the objective to f = 17858 (the true optimum) in ~300 s (vs the 169.7 s / viol 0.60 / f 8921 default) — a decisive opt-in win. On the TopOpt SAND tutorial it fires but cannot close the equilibrium gap: the raw merit Σ‖Ku−f‖² is a KᵀK least-squares whose SIMP stiffness conditioning κ(K)² stalls any first-order restore at resid ~0.08 (only the nested solve u = K⁻¹f — the NAND formulation from the SAND literature — or second-order steps reach equilibrium there).
  • restore_prox: (opt-in) Rockafellar anchor weight τ on the restoration objective: min Σ‖c‖² + Σ‖g⁺‖² + τ/2‖z − x̄‖² with x̄ the pre-restoration iterate — the violation merit alone can wander along its zero manifold; τ > 0 keeps the restored point close to the previous solution. The anchor is a per-variable scaled pseudo-Huber (log-cosh) penalty: each variable is scaled by its box width, and the anchor gradient is τ·tanh(Δᵢ/wᵢ) — magnitude below τ for every variable, saturating instead of growing linearly. 0 (default) disables the anchor.
  • keep_all: keep the trajectory of outer iterates in the result.

AugLagResult

The result of an AugLagAlg optimization: the GenericResult-style fields minimizer and minimum, the (currently nothing) sub-solver results, and trajectory — the vector of outer-loop iterates (x, f(x), ‖v(x)‖∞) when AugLagOptions(keep_all = true), nothing otherwise.

CompositeHessianApprox(qn, build_terms)

A Hessian model summing a quasi-Newton approximation qn (of the remainder) and a tuple of exact curvature terms rebuilt at every subproblem iterate via build_terms(x) (the Gauss-Newton penalty terms of the AL objective, or the label-selected exact terms). solve! is the quasi-Newton inverse (the CG preconditioner role).

DenseBFGSApprox(form = :inverse)

Dense BFGS. With form = :inverse (default) the inverse Hessian approximation is stored and updated, so solve! is a plain matrix-vector product; with form = :hessian the direct Hessian is stored and solve! factorizes it.

NonconvexAugLagLab.DiagonalPreconditioner — Type
DiagonalPreconditioner(d)

Diagonal Hessian scaling with entries d (all positive). solve! divides by the scaling, apply! multiplies by it.

NonconvexAugLagLab.FDGradHessianApprox — Type
FDGradHessianApprox(gradfun)

Inexact-Newton curvature from a one-sided finite difference of the (Zygote-computed) gradient: Hv = (g(x + εv) − g(x)) / ε — the directional derivative of the gradient, reusing the driver's current gradient so each Hessian-vector product costs a single extra gradient evaluation (Zygote cannot be nested, and ForwardDiff cannot cross its tape). Intended with a small CG budget (cg_maxiter).

NonconvexAugLagLab.GaussNewtonTerm — Type
GaussNewtonTerm(c, w, x[, act])

The Gauss-Newton term w · J(x)ᵀ(J(x) v) of one (scaled) constraint group, computed matrix-free: Jv by a forward finite difference from the cached value c(x) (one constraint evaluation) and Jᵀu by one reverse pass. IdentityPreconditioner()

Identity Hessian scaling: apply!/solve! copy their argument.

NonconvexAugLagLab.JumpGaussNewtonTerm — Type
JumpGaussNewtonTerm(je, rows, w, x[, act])

The Gauss-Newton term w · J(x)ᵀ(J(x) v) of a JuMP-converted constraint block: the exact sparse Jacobian of the MathOptInterface evaluator is materialized once per rebound point and each matvec is two sparse products — no finite differences and no reverse passes, so the per-CG-iteration cost is negligible (the Jacobian evaluation dominates, once per subproblem iterate).

NonconvexAugLagLab.JumpLagrangianTerm — Type
JumpLagrangianTerm(je, μ, x)

The exact multiplier-weighted constraint-curvature term Σ μᵢ ∇²cᵢ of a JuMP-converted model: the MathOptInterface evaluator's Lagrangian Hessian is assembled once per rebound point from its sparse triplets (σ = 0, so only the constraint part contributes) and each matvec is one sparse product. μ is the full evaluator-length multiplier vector in its row order (inequalities first, then equalities), zero outside the selected block.

Limited-memory BFGS. Stores the last memory secant pairs and applies the approximation (and its inverse) with the two-loop recursion; H0 is the base operator used at the bottom of the recursion and serves as the preconditioner.

NonconvexAugLagLab.LimitedMemorySR1Approx — Type
LimitedMemorySR1Approx(memory; H0 = IdentityPreconditioner())

Limited-memory symmetric rank-one. Stores the last memory pairs and applies B v = B0 v + Σ_k u_k (u_k'v)/(u_k's_k) with u_k = y_k − B0 s_k (the fixed-base L-SR1); pairs failing the safeguard |s'u| ≥ 1e-8 ‖s‖‖u‖ are rejected. Intended for globalization = :trustregion subproblems, where an indefinite Hessian model is desirable.

NonconvexAugLagLab.NewtonTRApprox — Type
NewtonTRApprox()

Exact Hessian of the (augmented Lagrangian) objective at every iterate, differentiated through NonconvexCore.value_gradient. Used together with QuasiNewtonAlg(; globalization = :trustregion) for the Newton trust-region method (Steihaug–Toint CG).

NonconvexAugLagLab.QuasiNewtonAlg — Type
QuasiNewtonAlg(approx = LBFGSApprox(10); globalization = :backtracking,
               max_iter = 200, tol = 1e-6, damping = true, delta0 = 1.0)

A smooth box-constrained quasi-Newton optimizer: approx is any AbstractHessianApprox (L-BFGS with an optional preconditioner, dense or structured BFGS, or the exact-Hessian Newton trust region). globalization is :backtracking (Armijo), :wolfe (strong Wolfe), :trustregion (Steihaug–Toint with a projected step) or :tron (the reference Lin–Moré TRON structure mirroring JSOSolvers: a safeguarded projected-Cauchy starting point with the search parameter carried across iterations, projected CG on the free set with a reduced-gradient exit, a projected line search on the free face, and the TRONTrustRegion radius rules; cg_maxiter caps the face iterations and cg_tol (default 0.1) the reduced-CG accuracy; works with every curvature model, first-order and exact-Hessian alike). damping enables Powell damping of the secant pairs for nonconvex objectives. projected_stop (default true) terminates on the box-projected gradient norm (the correct stationarity measure when the optimum sits on a bound, where the raw gradient does not vanish) instead of the raw gradient norm, and reports that projected norm as the KKT residual. Measured on the TopOpt local-stress single-level solve: bit-for-bit identical trajectory at 7.2% less wall-clock (303.1 vs 326.7 s); the reference TRON there does not complete within 13 min vs the 4.5 min :trustregion default (its per-step Cauchy/line-search matvecs dominate on FEA-heavy constraints), so :tron stays opt-in.

NonconvexAugLagLab.RelaxConstraintSelection — Type
RelaxConstraintSelection(; eq = true, ineq = true, sd = true, sets = true,
                          labels = nothing)

Selects which constraints are relaxed into the augmented Lagrangian. Each kind entry is true (relax all of that kind), false (keep all hard) or a vector of constraint indices (relax those only). In addition, labels (a Symbol or a collection of Symbols) relaxes every constraint whose flags (set with add_ineq_constraint!(m, f, s; flags = [:relax]) and friends) contain one of the given labels — independently of the kind selection. Box constraints are always handled by the sub-solver.

sets selects the function-in-set constraints (NonconvexCore.FunctionInSet). Relaxing one splits its components into the equivalent classical constraints (equality components as equalities, order/simplex/chance components as inequalities, PSDCone components as semidefinite constraints handled by the projected augmented Lagrangian — which requires NonconvexSemidefinite to be loaded); the split components are then always relaxed.

RelaxConstraintSelection(eq = false, ineq = false, sd = false, sets = false, labels = [:relax])

# all equalities, plus anything flagged :relax
RelaxConstraintSelection(labels = :relax)
NonconvexAugLagLab.SecondOrderSelection — Type
SecondOrderSelection(; eq = true, ineq = true, labels = nothing)

Selects which relaxed constraint groups contribute their exact multiplier-weighted curvature μᵀ∇²c to the hybrid Hessian model (curvature = :hybrid). Each kind entry is true (all groups of that kind), false (none) or a vector of group indices (those only); labels selects every constraint whose flags contain one of the given labels. Untagged groups keep only the Gauss-Newton penalty term and their multiplier curvature is absorbed by the quasi-Newton remainder. Only JuMP-converted blocks support the exact term (MathOptInterface Lagrangian-Hessian triplets).

NonconvexAugLagLab.SplitSecantApprox — Type
SplitSecantApprox(qn_rem, qn_pen, grad_penalty)

The split-secant model for problems without exact Hessians: the AL objective splits into the penalty part φ = ρ/2‖c‖² + ρ/2‖g⁺‖² and the remainder ψ = f + yᵀc + λᵀg⁺, each modeled by its own quasi-Newton history. The penalty model receives the secant pair (s, ∇φ(x⁺) − ∇φ(x)) — the exact directional second derivative of the dominant penalty curvature from gradient differences alone (two reverse passes of the scalar penalty function per accepted step, no Hessian-vector products) — and the remainder model receives the structured-secant corrected pair (s, y − Δ∇φ) (Dennis-Gay-Welsch in gradient-difference form). The CG model is the sum of the two two-loop recursions, and the penalty model's inverse is the Steihaug preconditioner (the secant inverse of the dominant term — the correct scaling, as opposed to the remainder inverse measured harmful on case5).

NonconvexAugLagLab.StructuredBFGSApprox — Type
StructuredBFGSApprox(B; form = :hessian)

BFGS with secants accumulated in a user-defined Hessian container B (e.g. a sparse matrix carrying a sparsity pattern, or any custom matrix type). The built-in update is the structure-preserving sparse BFGS (Fletcher): the dense BFGS update is restricted to the nonzero pattern of B, so the structure and the user's solve!/apply! implementations keep working. Custom matrix types should overload init!/update!/solve!/apply! directly.
