Theory: barriers, chordal decomposition and re-parameterization

NonconvexSemidefinite collects the SDP-specific mathematics of the Nonconvex ecosystem. This page reviews the theory behind each piece.

The semidefinite cone and its barrier

A symmetric matrix $P \in \mathbb{S}^n$ is positive semidefinite ($P \succeq 0$) iff $v^\top P v \ge 0$ for all $v$. The function

\[F(P) = -\log\det P, \qquad \operatorname{dom} F = \{P \succ 0\},\]

is a self-concordant logarithmic barrier for the cone $\mathbb{S}_+^n$ (Nesterov–Nemirovskii): it is strictly convex on the interior, tends to $+\infty$ on the boundary, and its barrier parameter is $\nu = n$. The primal barrier method replaces the constrained problem

\[\min \; f(x) \quad \text{s.t.} \quad P(x) \succeq 0\]

with the sequence of unconstrained subproblems

\[\min_x \; f(x) - c_k \log\det P(x), \qquad c_k \to 0.\]

Each subproblem is solved strictly inside the cone (the barrier is infinite on the boundary), and the minimizers trace the central path $x^*(c)$, which converges to a solution of the original problem as $c \to 0$. The duality gap of the barrier subproblem is exactly

\[\text{gap} = c \cdot m,\]

where $m$ is the total number of barrier terms — for the SDP constraint $m = n$ (each eigenvalue contributes one unit). This identity is what the tol option of BarrierOptions exploits for early termination.

The generalized barrier

BarrierAlg applies the same principle to every inequality-like constraint kind:

ConstraintBarrier termBarrier parameter
inequality $g(x) \le 0$$-\log(-g(x))$$1$ per scalar entry
bound $l \le x_i \le u$$-\log(x_i - l) - \log(u - x_i)$$1$ per finite bound side
semidefinite $P(x) \succeq 0$$-\log\det P(x)$$n$

The log barrier is defined only on the strict interior. So that sub-solver line searches can probe outside, BarrierAlg continues each term by its C² Taylor extension beyond a threshold $-\varepsilon$:

\[-\log(-t) \;\leadsto\; -\log\varepsilon + \frac{t+\varepsilon}{\varepsilon} + \frac{(t+\varepsilon)^2}{2\varepsilon^2}, \qquad t \ge -\varepsilon.\]

The extension region must shrink with the barrier coefficient ($\varepsilon \sim c$): with a fixed $\varepsilon$, points far outside the interior have a finite barrier value that the vanishing $c$ no longer penalizes, and the subproblems leak through the constraint.

Coefficient schedules

The fixed schedule $c_{k+1} = \tau c_k$ ($\tau < 1$) is the textbook choice. The adaptive = true mode instead targets the requested gap within the remaining budget, $c_{k+1} = c_k \cdot \max(\tau, (\text{gap}_*/c_k m)^{1/(N-k)})$, which accelerates the final iterations.

Chordal decomposition

A graph is chordal if every cycle of length $\ge 4$ has a chord; its maximal cliques can be listed efficiently once a perfect elimination ordering is known, and a sparse Cholesky factorization (AMD ordering) of the sparsity pattern produces exactly such an ordering plus the filled pattern. The central theorem is due to Grone, Johnson, Sá and Wolkowicz (1984):

A matrix $P$ whose sparsity pattern is contained in the edges of a chordal graph is positive semidefinite **iff every principal submatrix $P_{C,C}$ indexed by a maximal clique $C$ is positive semidefinite.

chordal_decompose therefore replaces one large $n \times n$ semidefinite constraint with $k$ clique constraints of sizes $|C_1|, \ldots, |C_k|$. For a banded/chain pattern the cliques have size $2$, turning an $O(n^3)$ eigendecomposition into $O(n)$ trivial ones. The equivalence is exact (no relaxation error), which makes chordal decomposition the single most effective sparsity optimization for SDP.

Two practical consequences:

  • Barrier side. For a chordal-sparse $P \succ 0$ the determinant factorizes as $\det P = \prod_C \det P_{C,C} \,/\, \prod_S \det P_{S,S}$ (over cliques and their separators), so $-\log\det P = -\sum_C \log\det P_{C,C} + \sum_S \log\det P_{S,S}$. The clique-sum $\sum_C -\log\det P_{C,C}$ is itself a barrier for the chordal cone and drives the same feasible set; the separator correction recovers the exact central path.
  • Sparsity probing. aggregate_sparsity detects the structural dependence of each matrix entry on the variables with reverse-mode AD (union with the numerical support of $P(x_0)$, since AD cannot see constant terms). User-supplied patterns are validated against $P(x_0)$: an entry outside the pattern would make the decomposition inequivalent.

Projections onto the PSD cone

The orthogonal projection onto $\mathbb{S}_+^n$ clamps eigenvalues:

\[\Pi_{\mathbb{S}_+^n}(A) = U \max(\Lambda, 0) U^\top, \qquad A = U\Lambda U^\top.\]

By the Moreau decomposition of mutually polar cones, $A = \Pi_{\mathbb{S}_+^n}(A) + \Pi_{\mathbb{S}_-^n}(A)$, which gives project_psd_neg for free; psd_infeasibility is then the norm of the negative-eigenvalue part, i.e. the distance of $P$ to the cone. The projection is a spectral function and is differentiable wherever no eigenvalue is pinned at zero; its differential (Lewis) is

\[D\Pi(A)[E] = U\, [G \circ (U^\top E U)]\, U^\top, \qquad G_{ij} = \begin{cases} (\bar\lambda_i - \bar\lambda_j)/(\lambda_i - \lambda_j), & \lambda_i \neq \lambda_j, \\ \mathbf{1}_{\lambda_i > 0}, & \lambda_i = \lambda_j, \end{cases}\]

with $\bar\lambda_i = \max(\lambda_i, 0)$. project_psd ships this pullback as a ChainRules rrule, so reverse-mode AD differentiates through the projection (e.g. inside the augmented Lagrangian penalty of NonconvexAugLagLab) with machine-precision gradients.

Quadratic re-parameterization

SDPQuadAlg eliminates the semidefinite constraint by substituting a factorization of the matrix:

  • Full rank: $A = LL^\top$ with lower-triangular $L$ and $\operatorname{diag}(L) \ge \varepsilon$. Every PSD matrix admits such a factor (Cholesky), so the substitution is surjective: no solution is lost. The subproblem is a box-constrained NLP over the $n(n+1)/2$ entries of $L$.
  • Low rank (Burer–Monteiro): $A = RR^\top$ with $R \in \mathbb{R}^{n\times r}$, $r \le n$. This covers exactly the PSD matrices of rank $\le r$; for large problems whose solutions are provably low rank (e.g. by Pataki's bound $\binom{r+1}{2} \le m$) it reduces the dimension from $O(n^2)$ to $O(nr)$.

Both substitutions make the problem smooth and unconstrained (up to the diagonal bound), at the cost of nonconvexity in the factor: the objective is invariant under orthogonal transformations $L \mapsto LQ$, so the factor problem has symmetries and possibly spurious stationary points. The diagonal floor keeps the full-rank factor strictly interior; for nonconvex objectives a multistart over factors is recommended.
